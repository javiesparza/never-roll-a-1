About Never Roll a 1

The Game

Never Roll a 1 is a simple yet addictive probability game. Your mission? Roll a standard six-sided die 10 times without hitting a 1. Sounds easy, right?

With a theoretical 16.15% chance of success per attempt (approximately), most attempts end before ten safe rolls. This is a mathematical probability, not a measured percentage of players. A single die and a single rule make it easy to explore repeated independent events.

The Math

Each roll has a 5/6 chance of success (any number except 1). To win, you need to succeed 10 times in a row:

(5/6)^10 ≈ 0.1615 or 16.15%

This game is a perfect demonstration of how probabilities compound. Even though each individual roll seems favorable, the odds stack against you quickly! For a comprehensive breakdown of the mathematics, including probability tables, win streak odds, and the gambler's fallacy, check out our Probability Guide.

Why We Built This

This independent project explores how a simple interactive game can make probability easier to discuss. The original educational goal is to connect the experience of repeated dice rolls with the multiplication rule for independent events.

The game and companion guides illustrate compound probability, variation in small samples, and the gambler's fallacy: a past loss does not make the next roll more likely to succeed. They are learning materials, not a prediction of any player's results or a formally reviewed curriculum.

How It Works

Never Roll a 1 is a client-side web application built with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Dice outcomes use the browser's pseudorandom number generator via Math.random(), mapped to six faces. The animation is a visual effect, not a way to control the outcome. This is a probability demonstration, not a certified randomness system.

Tailwind CSS is compiled into a production stylesheet served from this site, rather than generated by a third-party CDN script. Game statistics, including wins, streaks, and attempt counts, are saved in your browser's localStorage when available. These local stats are tied to that browser and device. Optional Google sign-in and cloud leaderboard storage are separate from the browser-based dice game.

The Leaderboard

The leaderboard uses Google Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore. While you are signed in and have a leaderboard display name, wins automatically submit scores; there is no separate opt-in for each win. Public score records include your display name, account-linked user ID, streak, attempts, and timestamp, plus a date for daily scores. Scores are ranked by streak length and then fewer attempts.

Daily wins, attempts, best streak, and the current win streak and streak-attempt count reset at midnight UTC, including while the game remains open. The daily leaderboard uses the same UTC day boundary. All-time totals and best streak persist. A new day changes which daily scores are shown; it does not delete older cloud records. Other players can still change your ranking. You can play signed out to avoid submitting scores. Read the Privacy Policy before using sign-in.

Explore at Your Own Pace

  • Stay calm - panicking won't change the odds
  • Accept that most attempts will fail - that's statistics!
  • Compare your results with the theoretical odds, remembering that small samples vary.
  • Take a break whenever you want. No strategy or number of attempts guarantees a win.
  • Read the How to Play guide for detailed feature explanations
  • Check out Dice Facts for fun trivia while you take a break
